## Extensions proposed here

1. Apply preference-substitution analysis to controversy construction: a judge-agent can replace the parties' dispute with a machine-convenient dispute.
2. Extend bias analysis from verdicts to the full adjudicative pipeline: facts, credibility, issue framing, authority retrieval, norm attribution, application, remedy, and memory.
3. Treat presentation and data format as potential sources of procedural advantage, not merely evaluation noise.
4. Model adaptation by parties as procedural selection: rewarded submission traits spread even without formal procedural rules.
5. Connect feedback from decisions into memory, precedent, and future evaluation as a possible recursive lock-in mechanism.

## Candidate-new contribution

The strongest candidate is the combined mechanism:

> An adjudicative agent can transform machine readability into an informal procedural selection pressure, then convert its own outputs into future authority, producing recursive adjudicative lock-in.

This contribution will be narrowed if prior literature already integrates all three elements: machine-legibility differentials, strategic adaptation by parties, and recursive authority from agent-produced decisions.

## Strongest novelty objection

The proposal may repackage established concerns about digital divide, automation bias, algorithmic discrimination, procedural fairness, and feedback loops under new labels.

## Required answer

The manuscript must show that the distinctive unit is not any one bias. It is the sequence by which representation differences become procedural payoffs and later become part of the authority environment for subsequent decisions.
