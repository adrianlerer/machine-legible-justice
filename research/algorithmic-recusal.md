# Algorithmic Recusal as a Research Extension

## Status

`CANDIDATE CONCEPT / NOT A CLAIM OF CURRENT LAW`

## Core proposition

An AI evaluator or adjudicative agent could be subject to a functional impartiality challenge when a properly attributed body of prior outputs reveals a reproducible pattern of relying on legally irrelevant features. “Recusal” is an illuminating analogy, but its legal availability, decision-maker, burden and remedy depend on the applicable jurisdiction and institutional design.

## What is challenged

The relevant object may be narrower or broader than a base model:

1. a model and exact version;
2. a configuration, including prompts, parameters and tools;
3. a retrieval corpus or memory state;
4. a deploying institution and its incentives;
5. use in one proceeding.

This distinction matters because prior outputs cannot establish a pattern unless they are attributable to the system now being challenged.

## Evidentiary ladder

- `INCIDENT`: one anomalous or erroneous output.
- `SIGNAL`: repeated concern, but without adequate comparability or controls.
- `REPLICATED PATTERN`: comparable cases and controlled counterfactuals reproduce the disparity.
- `NOT ATTRIBUTABLE`: the relevant model, configuration, sources or memory cannot be identified.

Prior outputs should be compared by task, governing source set, language, procedural posture, model version and time. A persuasive collection of anecdotes is not the same as a controlled pattern.

## Possible grounds

- systematic disparity in outcomes under legally equivalent inputs;
- process disparity in fact selection, credibility, issue framing, authority ordering, norm attribution, burden or remedy;
- recursive reliance on the system's own earlier summaries or recommendations;
- undisclosed institutional or data conflicts;
- lack of traceability sufficient to contest the result.

Outcome parity does not rule out process disparity. Conversely, a single adverse outcome does not prove structural bias.

## Proportional remedies

The response need not be binary. Possible remedies include disclosure, preservation of the full run record, counterfactual replay, independent evaluation, exclusion of contaminated memory, alternate configuration, quarantine, replacement, and human de novo review from the original record.

For decisions affecting rights, sanctions, money, liberty or legal status, automated reliance remains `BLOCK` absent lawful authority, an adversarial process, an inspectable record, an effective opportunity to challenge the system, and a competent human reviewer who can genuinely disregard its output.

## Research questions

1. What minimum stability of model identity is required before prior outputs can be attributed to the current system?
2. Should the challenge attach to the model, the deployment, the institution, or the proceeding?
3. Which process disparities justify disqualification even when final outcomes converge?
4. How should version changes affect the temporal relevance of prior outputs?
5. When does self-reuse of model-generated material become recursive adjudicative lock-in?
